The Methodology

We didn't find a system. We eliminated everything that wasn't one.

1,000,000+
combinations tested
219
signals survived

Three seasons of history, and every variable we could measure — kickoff window, day of the week, conference, week of the season, line size, how far our models sat from the market — crossed with the situational spots bettors argue about: rest, travel, rankings, coaching changes, streaks, revenge games. For spreads and totals that made 31,000 candidate signals. For the moneyline, where who wins is a different question than who covers, the search ran past a million candidate patterns over five million graded outcomes. Every one was graded point-in-time, at the line as it stood, then tested against random chance.

Almost everything died. 173 spread-and-total signals and 46 moneyline patterns survived — 219 in all. Those are the voters: a pick's grade, C up to A+, is simply how many of them agree on it. And underneath, four models rebuilt from scratch — opponent-adjusted, zero fudge factors, every backtest that looked too good torn down until we found the bug.

One layer sits on top and never votes: the streak marker. When a team's recent form against our projections adds an extra scenario that aligns with the standard signals, the pick wears a small bolt — a marker on the grade, never part of it.

That's what a grade is made of. What it earns gets settled in public.

The Life of a Pick

From release to the record — one frozen number.

A record only means something if you can't move the goalposts. Here's the whole lifecycle of every pick.

01
It releases once three books post a line
A pick goes live the moment at least three sportsbooks are posting that market. Right there we freeze the released line and tier — that frozen number is the bet of record, and the journal starts.
02
It re-grades every 30 minutes until kickoff
Every half hour, every pick is re-checked against the current best line at every book. The tier can climb, hold, or fall to No Edge as the market moves — and every change lands in the pick's journal, with the best book re-named each time.
03
It locks at kickoff, exactly as released
When the game starts, the pick leaves the board and locks — tier, line, price, and book, as released. Nothing about it can change once the ball is in the air.
04
It settles against the released line, in public
When the game goes final: win, loss, or push, scored against the line we released — not the closing line, not whichever number looks best after the fact. If we released Oklahoma +2.5, we're graded on +2.5. The settled pick posts to Results, linked to its game page.

We grade ourselves on the number we gave you — not the one that would've looked best.

The Model

Five reads. One verdict per bet.

SP+ — Bill Connelly's efficiency model. Adjusts for opponent strength.

Elo — Head-to-head record over time. Context only — it never fires a pick.

PPA — Expected points per play. The drive-by-drive read.

Advanced — Success rate, explosiveness, finishing drives. The "how" of efficiency.

Pace+ — Our in-house model. Possession elasticity weighted by opponent strength.

A pick doesn't start because the models average out a certain way. It starts when a model's number lands in a proven spot — a specific combination of model, market, and line range that survived the three-season test — and usually several signals fire on the same side at once. The grade counts that agreement across the 219 survivors. Four models carry signals; Elo never does — it's context you see, not a vote. And when nothing proven lines up, we don't manufacture a lean — that market publishes as No Edge.
