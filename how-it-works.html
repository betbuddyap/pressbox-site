How It Works

You're in.
Here's how to use it.

Start with the grades — they're the whole product in one letter. Then the four surfaces that carry them, the game page underneath, and the ledger where your own bets land: the full journey from released pick to settled number.

01 — The grades

What each grade means.

Every grade is a count of independent agreement — how many of our proven signals fired on the same side of the same bet, minus any that fired against it. Two signals is a C. Eight or more is an A+. A lone signal with nothing backing it doesn't clear the bar and grades No Edge. On the spread, one more voice speaks after the count: the engine, our play-by-play simulation. When it lands on the same side as the signals, the bet grades A+ whatever the count. When it lands on the other side, the bet comes off the board. The signals were found and tested across 2023–25; the grades they earn now are settled in public, game by game, on Results. Every grade is a published verdict, including the ones that say "pass."

One voice on its own didn't beat the vig across three seasons of testing, so we don't put your money behind it. We publish that verdict too, right on the board, next to the games we did bet.

Then the engine speaks — spread only

Underneath the signals sits the engine: a play-by-play simulation that plays every game thousands of times from the same team numbers. It does not vote with the signals — it checks them, and only when its own number sits far enough from the market, on the spread and the total together, to count as a real opinion. Totals and moneylines it leaves to the signals. Three things can happen.

Moneyline

The moneyline runs on its own signal set — rebuilt from scratch for 2026. Instead of borrowing spread signals, we searched the same three seasons for patterns that predict who wins — a different question than who covers — and only the survivors made the book. The grades count independent agreement here too, with one guard: moneyline signals that come from the same model share a single vote, so the count can't be padded by one model repeating itself.

A+All four models
Every model in the book landed on the same team to win outright, with nothing pulling the other way.
AGold — three models
Three of the four on the same side after disagreements net out.
BSilver — two models
Two clear votes for the same winner.
CBronze — one model
A single model's vote, unopposed. It clears the bar here — unlike on the spread, where one lone signal grades No Edge.

Same letters, different arithmetic — and that's deliberate. A spread grade counts agreement across 253 signals, so it takes eight to reach A+. A moneyline grade counts agreement across four models, so four is A+. An A+ moneyline means every model agreed; it does not mean the same thing as an A+ spread, and we'd rather say so than quietly re-letter one to match the other. The two answer different questions — a spread asks who covers, a moneyline asks who wins. And we only publish a moneyline priced between −300 and +300; outside that band the payout math stops being worth your money.

The bolt. Some picks carry a small bolt on the badge — our streak marker. It fires when a team's recent form sits in a band the market has historically mispriced and our pick already points the same way. It never changes a grade and never fires a pick on its own; the game page explains each one in plain words.

The chain. A second marker, on trial for 2026. The Chain is a fifth model built from nothing but this season's margins and opponents, so it cannot speak until both teams have three games. It looks at each game once, at the first line it sees, and fires when it sits three to six points from the market. It never votes and never changes a grade. When its side matches a graded pick, the badge wears the chain link; when it disagrees, the pick stands and the Chain is graded on its own. Every fire is on the game page and in its own record, win or lose.

Upcoming — the slate

Upcoming is the other door: every FBS-vs-FBS game as a card — kickoff time on top, the matchup in the middle, and the grades on the front, one slot each for Spread, Total, and ML. A badge in a slot means that bet is live on the board; an empty slot means that market has nothing yet. Scan the week, spot the games that matter, tap any card for its game page.

When a game kicks off, its card becomes a scoreboard, and every pick on it is tracked live. The score takes over as the headline with a pulsing LIVE tag, the quarter and clock, down and distance, and a possession dot beside the team with the ball. Beside each pick a ▲ or ▼ says, right now, whether it is on track to cash or running behind — the grade and the number stay locked as released, but the tracking never stops. At the final whistle the card flips to its gold final state, a ✓ or ✕ lands on every pick, finals sink to the bottom of the slate, and the graded picks post to Results.

04 — The deep view

The game page.

Tap "full game breakdown" on any bet and you land on the deepest view on the site. It's where the grade gets explained — in writing, in the engine's simulated range of outcomes, in the signals that voted, and in the numbers underneath.

  • A written breakdown
    A plain-English read on the matchup — what the models see and why the bet landed where it did.
  • The range of outcomes
    Every final margin the engine played out, stacked into one chart — with the market line on it, each model's number marked, and our side shaded gold. The total gets the same chart, drawn tall. More on this just below.
  • The game path
    Where the margin sits minute by minute across those simulated games: the median game as a gold line, the bands where most of them run, and a switch to see how the games go when either team scores first.
  • Team & advanced stats
    The numbers behind the call — efficiency, tempo, and the advanced splits the box score misses.
  • Head-to-head history
    How these two have met before.
  • The history check
    Under each chart, the real record of the signals behind the pick — how many back it, and how the side they pointed to actually did across the games they fired on. The same evidence that prices the pick, in one sentence.
  • Form vs our projections
    The Overperformance Index — how far each team has run above or below what we projected, in points per game. When a pick carries the streak marker, this is the section that explains it in plain words.
  • Live scoring — then the final state
    Once the game kicks off, the page goes live: the score tracks in real time up top, and a rust marker lands on every chart showing exactly where the game sits against the number right now — tagged LIVE while it runs, FINAL once it's done. The picks stay locked as released the whole way. After the final whistle the page freezes as the permanent record — grade, number, result — and Results links back to it.

The range of outcomes is the heart of it. The engine plays the game thousands of times and stacks every final margin into one chart — tall bars where the game most often lands, thin tails where it rarely does. The market line sits on that chart, each of the four models puts its number on the same axis, and the side we're on is shaded gold. A pick exists because proven signals fired on that side — usually several at once — and the grade is their net agreement; the panel under the pick names each one, shows the vote, and states the engine's read in a sentence: with the signals, against them, or too close to the market to weigh in. When nothing proven fires, the market grades No Edge — published all the same.

Then the game path: the margin minute by minute across every simulated game, the median game as a gold line, the bands where half and eight in ten of them run. Switch it to the games where either team scored first and read how the rest of those games tend to go — who leads at the half, how often the half-time leader holds on, when the winning lead usually gets taken.

05 — Parlay

Stack the edges.

The Parlay page turns graded picks into tickets. Grab pre-built cards, or build your own from the full board — every leg is priced against the current best market line, and the card shows two numbers side by side: our hit probability (each leg's win probability multiplied through) and Vegas's implied (the probability the ticket's own price says it needs). The gap between them is the whole point.

The three pre-built cards are one dial, turned three ways. Safest counts only how often legs hit. Best weighs how often against how much — the strongest value per dollar. Longshot weighs the payout double, so genuine dogs top the ticket while the grade still breaks the near-ties. Each card names the book paying the most for the whole ticket, with every book's price behind it.

06 — Allocator

Your bankroll, split for you.

Tell the Allocator how much you're putting in play. It sizes every bet on the board by how far our win probability sits above what the price requires, weighted by how often the bet actually cashes — so a strong edge at a fair price gets the biggest slice, a thin edge gets a sliver, and anything at or below its price gets nothing at all. Bets that don't earn a stake simply drop off the sheet.

Conservative, Moderate, Aggressive tilt the same math toward steadier bets or bigger payouts. Toggle in a parlay or two if you want them in the mix. And if you only use one sportsbook, pick it at the top — every stake re-sizes at that book's lines and juice, picks it doesn't post disappear, and every bet button points where you actually bet. The sheet ends with the total to have at each book.

When the sheet is how you want it, send it to My Bets. The whole allocation lands in your ledger as editable rows — every side, book, price, and stake already filled in. Correct anything the book gave you differently, drop what you didn't place, and it's logged. That's the handoff: the Allocator decides how much, My Bets records what you actually did.

07 — My Bets

Your money, your ledger.

My Bets is the one page on this site that isn't about our picks — it's about yours. Log a bet in a few taps: week, game, spread or total or moneyline, which side, which book, the price you got, the amount. Multi-leg parlays too. It's private to your login and nobody else sees it, including us in any form that's tied to you.

You're graded on your number, not ours. If we published Oklahoma +3 and you got +3.5, your ledger settles at +3.5 — the line you actually bet, from the side you actually took. That's the same rule we hold ourselves to on Results, pointed at your slip instead of our board. Where we have a pick on a game you've logged, the row shows which side we're on, so you can see when you're with us and when you're on your own.

It goes live when the games do. Leave it open on your phone during the slate and every ticket carries its current standing — a mark and a plain-words reason, like a red ✕ with "USC down 10" on a moneyline, or a green ✓ with "total 15 under the line" on an under. Parlays roll up to the ticket: one leg gone is the ticket gone. When a game goes final the mark settles, the profit and loss lands, and the season total moves.

You can delete a bet right up until its game kicks off. After that it's part of the record — which is the point of keeping one.

08 — Results & Rankings

The record, and the map.

Results is Live Lines for games that are over. When a game kicks off its picks leave the board; when it goes final they land there — the released grade on the badge, the pick at the number it was graded on, a ✓, ✕, or – next to each, and the final score up top. Every card links back to the game page's final state. Graded no-edge verdicts appear too, dimmed — published, but never counted in the record, because they were never bets.

Rankings is the engine pointed at the season: every FBS team, ranked by the margin the simulation expects against an average team over thousands of played-out games — the "who's actually good" view behind every number on the board.

09 — After the game

We're graded on the number we gave you.

When a bet releases, we freeze the line and tier we published. It re-grades against the live market every 30 minutes and can slip to No Edge as the line moves. At kickoff it locks as released. When the game ends, win or loss is scored against the number we released — not a friendlier closing line. Every result lands on Results, win or lose, next to the games we passed on.
